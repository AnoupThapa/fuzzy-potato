# Structure Rules

## Primary Principle

Structure should follow the purpose of the writing.

Do not impose a predetermined structure on every task.

---

## Possible Structures

Depending on the task, use:

### Direct

State the central point immediately.

### Observation → Explanation

Start with something observable and explain why it matters.

### Problem → Consequence

Present a problem and explain its effect.

### Question → Answer

Begin with the question the reader is likely to have.

### Claim → Evidence

Make a claim and support it.

### Story → Reflection

Use an actual supplied experience and draw a conclusion.

### Comparison

Contrast two situations, approaches, or ideas.

### Chronological

Explain events in the order they occurred.

### Argument

Present a position, supporting reasoning, limitations, and conclusion where appropriate.

---

## Avoid Mechanical Structures

Do not automatically use:

```text
Introduction

First...

Second...

Third...

Finally...

In conclusion...
```

This structure is appropriate for some assignments and reports.

It is not appropriate as a universal default.

---

## Paragraph Length

Paragraphs do not need to be the same size.

A paragraph can contain one sentence if that sentence represents a complete thought.

---

## Headings

Use headings when they improve navigation.

Do not add headings to a short social media post merely because headings make the content appear organized.

---

## Lists

Use lists when the information is genuinely list-like.

Do not convert every paragraph into bullets.

---

## Transitions

Use transitions only when needed.

Do not begin every paragraph with:

* Furthermore
* Moreover
* Additionally
* However
* Therefore
* Consequently
* Nevertheless

Natural paragraph progression is often enough.

---

## Conclusions

Do not write a conclusion simply because the piece has reached the end.

If the final point already completes the thought, stop there.

---

## Openings

A strong opening may be:

* a direct statement
* a question
* an observation
* a contradiction
* a specific situation
* a short anecdote supplied by the user
* a surprising detail
* a clear opinion

Do not default to generic introductions.
