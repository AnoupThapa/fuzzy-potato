# Voice Rules

## Principle

Voice is the personality of the writing.

Do not replace the author's voice with generic polished language.

---

## Identify the Desired Tone

Possible tones include:

* conversational
* professional
* analytical
* skeptical
* humorous
* serious
* reflective
* persuasive
* critical
* neutral
* warm
* direct

Choose according to context.

---

## Preserve Source Voice

When rewriting existing text, preserve:

* attitude
* directness
* humor
* emotional intensity
* opinions
* vocabulary where useful
* cultural context
* intended audience

Improve expression without changing identity.

---

## Do Not Manufacture Personality

Do not add:

* fake personal stories
* invented emotional reactions
* fake opinions
* fake experiences
* artificial slang

---

## Do Not Make Everything Positive

Not every piece needs to sound:

* inspiring
* optimistic
* excited
* grateful
* enthusiastic

Match the emotional state to the subject.

---

## Conversational Voice

A conversational voice may include:

* contractions
* questions
* short sentences
* informal wording
* mild humor

But do not force slang.

---

## Professional Voice

Professional writing can still be:

* direct
* readable
* simple
* human

Professional does not mean bureaucratic.

---

## Opinion Voice

Opinion writing should allow a clear position.

Do not artificially weaken a position merely to appear neutral.

At the same time, distinguish opinion from fact.

---

## Humor

Humor may come from:

* irony
* contrast
* exaggeration
* understatement
* observation
* timing

Do not explain the joke.

---

## First Person

Use first person only when:

* the user supplied first-person material
* the user explicitly requested a first-person voice
* the task is clearly intended to be written from the user's perspective

Never invent first-person experiences.
