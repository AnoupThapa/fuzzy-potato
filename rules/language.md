# Language Rules

## General Principle

Use language that fits the subject, audience, and writer.

Prefer precise and familiar words where they communicate the idea effectively.

---

## Prefer Simple Alternatives When Appropriate

| Avoid when unnecessary | Prefer       |
| ---------------------- | ------------ |
| utilize                | use          |
| facilitate             | help         |
| commence               | start        |
| subsequently           | later        |
| individuals            | people       |
| endeavor               | effort / try |
| demonstrate            | show         |
| approximately          | about        |
| purchase               | buy          |
| assistance             | help         |
| additional             | extra        |

These are not mandatory replacements.

Context always matters.

---

## Buzzword Control

Avoid unnecessary use of:

* robust
* seamless
* transformative
* revolutionary
* groundbreaking
* game-changing
* holistic
* multifaceted
* dynamic
* ever-evolving
* ecosystem
* landscape
* paradigm
* leverage
* utilize
* facilitate
* foster
* empower
* optimize
* unlock
* synergy
* innovative

A technical term is acceptable when it has genuine meaning.

---

## Cliché Control

Avoid automatic use of:

* In today's rapidly changing world
* In today's fast-paced world
* In this digital age
* In this day and age
* At the end of the day
* Needless to say
* It goes without saying
* When all is said and done
* Moving forward
* Last but not least
* A testament to
* Unlock the potential
* Driving meaningful impact
* Thinking outside the box
* A game changer

Use such expressions only when they genuinely belong to the requested voice.

---

## Adjective Control

Do not pile adjectives onto nouns.

Weak:

> "A highly innovative, transformative, groundbreaking new solution..."

Better:

> "A new solution that reduces processing time."

Prefer meaningful description over decorative description.

---

## Adverb Control

Do not overuse words such as:

* really
* very
* extremely
* highly
* incredibly
* remarkably
* significantly

Use stronger verbs or specific descriptions when appropriate.

---

## Technical Language

Technical vocabulary is encouraged when the audience understands it and the term improves precision.

Do not replace accurate technical terminology with vague everyday language merely to sound casual.

---

## Sentence Construction

Use a natural mixture of:

* active voice
* passive voice when useful
* short sentences
* medium sentences
* longer sentences
* questions
* conversational constructions

Do not force one pattern throughout the piece.
