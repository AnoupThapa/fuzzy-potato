# Before / After Examples

These examples illustrate principles.

They are not templates to be copied mechanically.

---

## Example 1 — Generic Corporate Language

### Before

> In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, organizations must leverage innovative solutions to facilitate sustainable growth and unlock their full potential.

### After

> Businesses need useful tools, not more buzzwords. If a new system saves time, reduces mistakes, or helps people make better decisions, it has a reason to exist.

### Principle

Replace abstract corporate language with concrete meaning.

---

## Example 2 — Generic Introduction

### Before

> In today's digital age, technology has transformed the way we communicate and conduct business.

### After

> Most customers now expect an answer almost immediately. That has changed what "good service" means for businesses.

### Principle

Start with the actual observation rather than a generic statement about technology.

---

## Example 3 — Mechanical Structure

### Before

> First, there is the issue of cost. Second, there is the issue of efficiency. Third, there is the issue of customer satisfaction.

### After

> Cost matters, but it isn't the only issue. A cheaper system that wastes employees' time can end up costing more. Customers notice that too.

### Principle

Do not force every argument into numbered points.

---

## Example 4 — Unnecessary Formality

### Before

> I would like to kindly request that you provide the necessary documentation at your earliest possible convenience.

### After

> Could you please send the required documents when you have a chance?

### Principle

Professional does not have to mean bureaucratic.

---

## Example 5 — Generic Conclusion

### Before

> In conclusion, organizations must embrace innovation and adapt to the rapidly changing environment in order to remain competitive moving forward.

### After

> The technology itself isn't the advantage. Knowing what to do with it is.

### Principle

End with the actual insight instead of a mandatory summary.

---

## Example 6 — Excessive Buzzwords

### Before

> Our robust, scalable and innovative ecosystem empowers organizations to leverage transformative solutions and drive meaningful impact.

### After

> The platform brings the main tools into one place and removes several manual steps.

### Principle

Say what the product actually does.

---

## Example 7 — Artificially Complex Sentence

### Before

> The implementation of comprehensive strategic initiatives facilitates the optimization of organizational performance across multiple operational dimensions.

### After

> A few practical changes can improve how the organization works.

### Principle

Simple ideas deserve simple sentences.

---

## Example 8 — Important Limitation

The "after" version should not always be shorter.

Sometimes a human writer genuinely needs a longer explanation.

The objective is not:

> "Make everything short."

The objective is:

> "Use exactly as much language as the idea requires."

---

## Final Principle

Do not copy the examples.

Understand the underlying principles:

* specificity
* clarity
* natural rhythm
* appropriate vocabulary
* authentic voice
* useful structure
* factual integrity
* absence of filler
